The changing shape of Russia's federal elections

Start with the precinct fingerprint, then trace turnout modes, regional incumbent support, round-number anomalies, and panel coverage. These views are exploratory summaries of RED, not legal judgments about individual polling stations.

Signature pattern

The precinct fingerprint changes shape as the system consolidates

Each cell counts polling stations by turnout (x) and incumbent share of registered voters (y). Smooth clusters, diagonal high-turnout/high-share tails, and integer-percentage bands are distributional anomaly indicators. They are not proof of fraud at any specific polling station. National trajectory

Presidential elections mobilize more voters; off-site voting grows over time

Turnout is shown as a share of registered voters. The second chart separates off-site ("home") and early voting, which are harder to observe than ordinary in-person voting.

Turnout = (valid + invalid ballots) / registered voters. Off-site and early voting shown as % of registered voters.
Regional variation

Incumbent support is regionally concentrated, not evenly national

Incumbent share of registered voters combines turnout and vote share. Pick a year to rank regions; click a bar or use the search box to see one region's full trajectory.

Incumbent = Putin (2000, 2004, 2012, 2018), Medvedev (2008), United Russia party list (2003–2021). Region names as reported in raw returns (unharmonized in this demo).
Forensic lens

Round-number spikes reveal non-random percentage patterns

Frequency of the tenths digit of precinct percentages. Under smooth reporting, each digit should appear near 10% of the time. A spike at 0 is consistent with administrative rounding, target-setting, manipulation, or other non-random reporting processes; it is an anomaly indicator, not proof for any polling station.

Digit taken from percentages rounded to one decimal; digit 0 ⇒ integer percentage. Dashed line = 10% uniform expectation.
