Usage notes

The dataset is panel-ready, but precinct identifiers, online-voting aggregates, coverage gaps, and forensic indicators require careful handling.

Use harmonized IDs

Build panels on uik and tik, not source-year identifiers.

Separate online voting

Remote e-voting rows are aggregates, not physical precincts.

Check coverage

Some years and territories require explicit exclusion notes.

Frame anomalies carefully

Forensic charts indicate patterns; they are not legal proof.

Panel analysis: use harmonized identifiers

Precinct (uik) and commission (tik) identifiers are harmonized to 2024 boundaries. Harmonized precinct codes may not match the actual numbers used in earlier years; originals are preserved in uik_old and tik_old. Always build panels on the harmonized IDs.

Online voting rows are not precincts

Remote e-voting results are appended as aggregated rows — at the electoral-district level for 2021 and regional level for 2024. turnout_online is non-zero only in these rows. Do not pool them with regular precinct rows in turnout analyses.

Known coverage gaps

Data for certain regions in 2000 may be incomplete (Chechnya most notably). Yakutia 2000 was recovered from a printed handbook. Territories outside Russia's internationally recognized borders are excluded. In 2024, RED captures 98.2% of the official electorate but 88.6% of official turnout, reflecting excluded territories and non-precinct voting modes.

Boundary changes vs renumbering

Harmonization resolves renumbering, splits, mergers, and renaming — but not precinct boundary redrawing, which is unobservable in the source data and may introduce measurement error in long panels.

Forensic indicators are indicators

Last-digit spikes, integer-percentage anomalies, and turnout–vote-share patterns shown in the dashboard are statistical anomaly indicators. They can be consistent with manipulation, administrative target-setting, reporting artifacts, or other non-random processes; they are not legal proof of fraud at any specific polling station.
