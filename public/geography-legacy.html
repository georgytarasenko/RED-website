Every polling station RED can place on the map

Explore RED across three spatial scales: every geocoded polling station, federal-subject summaries, and 25-year regional trajectories. Use valid-vote share for conventional results and registered-voter share when turnout and support should be read together. For a drill-down map with rayon detail and per-precinct panel history, open the Map Explorer.

Map-first view

1 · 85,000+ polling stations, mapped for 2024

Zoom and hover to inspect geocoded precinct returns; 2012, 2018, 2021, and 2024 point files are available in this demo. Online-voting aggregates are not shown because they are not physical polling stations.

Try this first Scroll to zoom, drag to rotate the globe. Hover a point for its party share, turnout, and electorate. City and region labels come from the basemap.
Source: RED v2.0 · geocoded from harmonized polling-station addresses (2012, 2018, 2021, 2024; 2016 not geocoded in this release). Globe projection via MapLibre GL; basemap © OpenFreeMap · © OpenMapTiles · © OpenStreetMap contributors. Online-voting aggregates excluded — they are not physical stations.

2 · Putin's 2024 support as a share of all registered voters

Each tile is one federal subject, arranged to preserve Russia's rough geography. % of valid votes is the conventional result; % of registered voters combines turnout and support; Δ vs previous election shows movement within the same election type.

Click a tile to add that region to the trajectory chart below. Online-voting rows are included in regional totals; hover for details.

3 · Regional differences narrow as the regime consolidates

Left: selected regions' trajectories for the chosen party. Right: the cross-regional standard deviation, a compact view of regional convergence over time.

4 · United Russia's regional map consolidates over time

Same tile layout, every election the party contested, on a fixed color scale. Try KPRF or Yabloko for shrinking opposition maps.

5 · Precinct result distributions expose bunching and tails

Histogram of a party's share of valid votes across all precincts. The default comparison shows Putin and Davankov in 2024; log scale reveals rare bins and excess bunching.

6 · High-turnout precincts tell different stories for incumbents and opposition

The 2D fingerprint generalized to any major party. Incumbent patterns and opposition patterns should be read as distributional indicators, not precinct-level verdicts.

7 · Small and large precincts reveal different support profiles

Party share by polling-station size. Size is a rough urban-rural proxy, not a direct settlement classification.
