The core contribution

A polling station keeps changing its number. RED keeps its identity.

Russia's Central Election Commission renumbers polling stations (UIKs) between elections, respells their addresses, splits and merges them. Every prior researcher reconciled this by hand, election by election. RED does it once — linking fragments into stable precinct_ids by address matching and sequence alignment — so that turnout, vote share, geography and anomaly indicators can all be followed across 25 years. Everything on the rest of this site is downstream of the work shown here.

Use panels safely

Key on precinct_id, not UIK

Raw UIK numbers are reused and reassigned. Join and lag on the harmonized precinct_id; keep uik_old to audit any link.

Start from the full panel

The 31,237 all-eleven precincts are the most robust base for fixed-effects and change models. Widen to shorter patterns as your design allows.

Respect splits & merges

Where a station splits or merges, continuity is flagged, not forced. Treat flagged links as measurement error, not clean identity.

Online voting isn't a precinct

Remote e-voting aggregates (2021 district, 2024 regional) are appended and flagged — never mixed into physical panels. See Usage Notes.
